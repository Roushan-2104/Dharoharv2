
Digital Heritage Knowledge System
Consolidated Project Vision, Features, Technology Direction, Methodology, Feasibility, Risks, and Implementation Principles
Smart India Hackathon 2026 — Idea Submission Reference Document
Prototype Scope: Odisha  |  Full Vision: India-wide

1. Problem Statement and SIH Alignment
Problem statement
Develop an interactive platform that connects monuments, historical personalities, festivals, art forms and regional traditions to create an accessible digital heritage knowledge system.
SIH theme alignment
Ideas that showcase the rich cultural heritage and traditions of India.
The concept aligns strongly with the SIH theme because it is centered on showcasing both tangible and intangible heritage. The project is not positioned as a generic travel app; travel-related functions support the cultural discovery experience.
Core framing
A connected, living heritage knowledge system rather than a static information website.
Connects monuments, personalities, festivals, art forms, traditions, food, local stories and lesser-known heritage.
Makes relationships between cultural elements explorable.
Combines formal/verified information with clearly labelled community knowledge.
Supports the journey: Discover → Learn → Experience → Contribute → Preserve.
2. Problems the Project Addresses
Fragmented heritage information
Heritage information is spread across government portals, museums, research publications, tourism websites, books, local communities and social platforms. Users often find isolated facts rather than the relationships between cultural elements.
Over-focus on famous destinations
Well-known monuments dominate online discovery while lesser-known sites, local traditions, folk practices and less-recognized historical personalities receive less attention.
Static consumption of heritage
Many heritage portals are primarily search-and-read experiences instead of interactive exploration systems.
Loss of local and oral knowledge
Important cultural knowledge exists within communities and may not be represented in formal datasets.
Accessibility barriers
Language, presentation style, technical vocabulary and lack of multimodal content can make heritage knowledge harder to access.
3. Core Project Vision
The platform treats heritage as a connected system rather than a collection of isolated records.
Konark Sun Temple → Narasimhadeva I → Kalinga architecture → Odissi → Konark Dance Festival → Traditional crafts → Local cuisine → Nearby heritage → Local stories
The intended product loop is:
DISCOVER → LEARN → EXPERIENCE → CONTRIBUTE → PRESERVE → (loops back to Discover)

14. Nearby Stay and Food Discovery
Shows hotels, guest houses, dhabas and restaurants around heritage locations using external places data; supports the visit rather than becoming a booking platform.

Interactive quizzes about monuments, history, festivals, art and traditions. Correct answers earn points.

Gamifies learning and exploration rather than assigning higher value to a monument simply because it is harder to reach.
19. Heritage Challenges
Goals such as discovering lesser-known monuments, learning art forms or exploring multiple regions.
20. Personalized Recommendations
Recommends places, stories, traditions, personalities or art forms based on the user's exploration history and interests.
21. "Because You Explored..." Discovery
Contextual rabbit-hole recommendations from one heritage topic to related sites, people, events, art and traditions.
22. What Else Is Around Here?
Surfaces culturally relevant nearby monuments, stories, food, festivals, crafts and hidden heritage around a selected location.
23. Digital Heritage Archive
Longer-term repository for text, images, audio, video, historical documents, stories, timelines and references.
24. Multimedia Heritage Storytelling
Uses images, video, audio narration, maps, timelines and stories instead of only paragraphs.
25. Regional Language Support
English, Hindi and regional Indian languages, including preferred-language AI interaction.
26. Accessibility
Text-to-speech, audio descriptions, high contrast, simplified explanations and accessible presentation.
27. Source and Provenance Layer
Shows whether information comes from official, research, verified contributor or community sources.
28. Source-aware AI
AI answers should be grounded in retrieved heritage information and identify when something is a local tradition, interpretation or uncertain claim.
29. Heritage Journey
Signature experience that connects history, people, places, traditions, food, festivals, stories, AI guidance, itinerary planning and learning into one journey.

5. Innovation and Uniqueness — Honest Assessment
The individual technologies and features are not inherently novel. Interactive maps, chatbots, recommendations, quizzes, weather and crowdsourced content already exist. Claiming that these features alone are revolutionary would weaken the pitch.
The stronger differentiation is the system design and cultural context:
Heritage as a connected network: Place → person → event → architecture → art → tradition → food → story → region.
Formal + community knowledge: combines structured/verified heritage information with local and oral knowledge, while keeping provenance visible.
Hidden heritage discovery: deliberately surfaces less-famous cultural assets rather than only optimizing for popular tourist destinations.
Living record: timelines, contributions and updates allow heritage to be represented as evolving rather than frozen.
Knowledge-to-experience loop: Learn → Discover → Plan → Visit → Contribute → Preserve.
Proposed one-line positioning
“We are building a living digital heritage network that connects India's monuments, people, traditions, art, stories and communities — turning fragmented heritage information into an interactive journey of discovery and preservation.”

A key principle is to keep the heritage dataset in your own PostgreSQL database. The map provider should be a visualization/geospatial layer, not the repository of your heritage knowledge.
Tech stack — re-examined and challenged
React: Holds up well for a map-heavy, component-driven, interactive UI.
Node.js/Express: Reasonable for I/O-heavy orchestration; needs enforced layered structure (routes/services/repositories) to avoid sprawl at this feature count.
PostgreSQL: Correct choice — relational data model fits site→person→art form→festival relationships. Neo4j/MongoDB considered and dismissed as unnecessary complexity at this scale. pgvector deferred until real semantic retrieval is implemented.
Mapbox: Justified for custom heritage-layer styling; open tension with also using Google Places — may mean paying for two mapping-adjacent APIs simultaneously.
Gemini + RAG: Prototype version is realistically simple DB/keyword retrieval, not true vector search — labelled honestly as “keyword/DB retrieval (prototype) → vector RAG (future)” so the pitch doesn't overstate what's built.
Why not Gemini “Gems”?
Gems are a consumer-facing Gemini app feature — not embeddable in a custom frontend, not connected to a live PostgreSQL database, and offering no control over conversation flow or UI integration. The project instead calls the Gemini API directly from the Node.js backend with a persona-style system prompt (“You are a heritage guide for Odisha...”), paired with real per-query retrieval from the project's own database — giving the same persona benefit as a Gem, with full grounding and integration a Gem cannot offer.

8. Methodology and Implementation Process
The overall methodology discussed is:
DATA SOURCES → COLLECTION → CURATION & VALIDATION → STRUCTURED HERITAGE DATABASE → RELATIONSHIP MAPPING → API / APPLICATION LAYER → MAP + SEARCH + AI + RECOMMENDATIONS → USER EXPERIENCE → COMMUNITY CONTRIBUTIONS → KNOWLEDGE BASE IMPROVEMENT
High-level system flow
Heritage Sources → Data Collection → Data Validation → PostgreSQL Heritage Database → Node.js API → [React UI: Mapbox (Explore Layers, Itinerary) + Gemini/RAG (AI Guide, Answers)] → Community Contributions → Knowledge Base
Relationship layer example
Narasimhadeva I —BUILT→ Konark Sun Temple
Konark Sun Temple —EXAMPLE_OF→ Kalinga Architecture
Konark Sun Temple —ASSOCIATED_WITH→ Konark Dance Festival
Konark Sun Temple —CONNECTED_TO→ Odissi
Odissi —PRACTICED_IN→ Odisha
Konark —NEAR→ Other Heritage Sites
9. AI / RAG Methodology
Gemini should not be treated as the source of truth. The intended approach is retrieval-grounded generation:
User Question → Query Processing → Retrieve relevant heritage records/source context → Gemini → Grounded response with provenance where possible.
For a future full-scale implementation, semantic/vector retrieval can be added. For the short prototype, a simpler database retrieval approach may be enough; there is no need to over-engineer the first demo.
Sample system prompt (persona layer)
You are the AI Heritage Guide for a digital heritage platform focused on Odisha, India. Explain cultural significance, history, and connections between heritage elements. Only state facts present in the provided CONTEXT; if the CONTEXT doesn't contain the answer, say so honestly. Clearly distinguish verified/official information from community or oral-tradition information when the CONTEXT indicates a source type. Keep responses warm and engaging — like a knowledgeable local guide, not a dry encyclopedia.
10. Itinerary Methodology
User Preferences (available time, interests, starting location, travel preferences, priority) → Candidate heritage sites → Filter and rank → Route/sequence generation → Personalized Heritage Journey → Mapbox visualization.
For the early implementation, this can be rule-based rather than ML-based. A simple score can combine interest match, cultural relevance, distance efficiency, time availability and user preference.

11. Community Contribution and Verification Methodology
User/Local contributor → Submit story/fact/photo/tradition → Metadata + source/context → Moderation/review → (Rejected/revision requested, OR) Community Contribution – Not Yet Verified → Visible to users with clear provenance → Potential researcher/historian review → Verified contribution.
The project should not promise perfect verification. Instead, it should make uncertainty and provenance visible.
12. Feasibility and Viability
Overall assessment: technically feasible, but data quality and content operations are the harder long-term problems than the software stack.
Technical feasibility — High for phased implementation
React, Node.js, PostgreSQL, Mapbox and Gemini are established technologies. The major engineering challenge is integration and data quality, not inventing new infrastructure.
Data feasibility — Moderate
A carefully curated regional dataset is practical. Nationwide coverage requires substantial sourcing, normalization, provenance management and likely institutional partnerships.
Operational feasibility — Moderate to High
The product needs only normal web/mobile access, but ongoing moderation, source checking, language work and API management grow with scale.
Economic feasibility — Moderate
An early prototype can rely on open-source tools and limited API usage. At scale, map, AI, places/weather APIs, storage and moderation create recurring costs.
Scalability — High at the architecture level
The architecture can expand from Odisha to more states. The main scaling challenge is maintaining a trustworthy, well-structured heritage dataset and community contribution workflow.

13. Potential Challenges and Risks
Risk
Why It Matters
Historical accuracy and misinformation
Community contributions or inconsistent sources may contain errors, exaggerated stories or disputed claims.
Data availability and standardization
Different sources may use different names, dates, spellings, categories or interpretations.
AI hallucination
Gemini may produce plausible but unsupported historical statements if it is not grounded in retrieved information.
Community moderation
Open contributions can introduce spam, duplicates, inappropriate content or deliberate misinformation.
Third-party API dependency
Pricing, quotas, policy changes or availability can affect Mapbox, Google Places, weather or Gemini integrations.
Mapping and route accuracy
Remote/less-known sites may have incomplete coordinates, roads or accessibility information.
Multilingual accuracy
Automatic translation can lose cultural nuance or mistranslate names and historical terms.
Content scalability
High-quality descriptions for thousands of entities require significant curation and maintenance.
Location/privacy risk
Future location-based functions must handle consent and retention responsibly.
Scope creep
Trying to build every feature at once can produce a technically broad but unconvincing prototype.

14. Strategies for Overcoming Challenges
Strategy
Purpose
Tiered verification
Use visible provenance levels such as official/institutional source, research/academic source, verified contributor, community contribution, and under review.
Curated core dataset
Start with a controlled trusted core. Keep crowdsourced content clearly separated instead of making every submission part of the trusted knowledge layer.
Grounded AI
Use retrieval before generation and provide source context. Distinguish documented history from oral tradition or uncertain interpretations.
Human-in-the-loop moderation
Send community submissions to review queues. Scale reviewer access to researchers, historians, institutions or trained moderators.
API abstraction
Access external services through the Node.js backend so providers can be replaced later without rewriting the frontend.
Open geographic formats
Store latitude/longitude and GeoJSON-style geographic data in your own database so the heritage dataset is not locked to a map provider.
Separate heritage and travel data
Keep core cultural knowledge in PostgreSQL while using external services for changing travel data such as hotels, restaurants, weather and routing.
Phased development
Expand from Odisha to more states and then India-wide rather than promising nationwide completeness immediately.
Privacy by design
Request explicit location permission, avoid unnecessary storage of precise location and provide clear controls for location features.

20. Expanded AI Usage — Beyond Conversational Q&A
The AI layer can do more than power a chatbot. The following uses extend Gemini's role across content production, moderation, and personalization:
AI-assisted content generation for curation: Gemini drafts first-pass entity summaries from raw source text (ASI records, Sahapedia entries), which a human curator edits/verifies — turns a 9-day content bottleneck into an editing task.
Automatic relationship extraction: Gemini proposes candidate relationships (site→person, art form→region) from raw text for human approval — seeds the Connected Heritage Knowledge graph faster.
Multilingual translation assistance: drafts Odia/Hindi translations of descriptions for human review, supporting Regional Language Support (Feature 25).
Image-based recognition: user photographs a monument → Gemini's vision capability identifies it and pulls up the connected heritage entry — a strong, demo-friendly differentiator.
Moderation assistance: flags likely spam, duplicates, or contradictory claims before human review, reducing reviewer workload.
Story/narrative generation for lesser-known sites: drafts engaging, clearly AI-assisted (not verified-fact) narrative framing for hidden heritage entries with sparse source data.
Personalization signal beyond rules: natural-language explanation layer on top of the rule-based itinerary engine (“because you explored Odissi, here's a temple linked to the same dance tradition”).
Quiz generation: auto-generates quiz questions from verified entity data instead of hand-writing all of them.
Honest prioritization for the 9-day prototype: content-generation uses (summaries, relationship extraction, quiz generation) give the most production leverage for the least engineering cost. Image recognition is the strongest demo differentiator if time allows. The rest are strong future-scope talking points.

21. Data Sources and References (India-wide)
Government / official heritage bodies
Source
What It Provides
Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)
Regulates and protects monuments/archaeological sites of national importance under the Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act, 1958; operates through 37 regional Circles. Maintains 3,757 Monuments of National Importance (2023 update).
State Protected Monuments (via ASI/state govts)
State-level protected sites, separate from nationally protected ones; varies widely by state (Karnataka 747, Gujarat 366, Odisha 218).
Ministry of Culture, Government of India
Apex body overseeing cultural institutions — IGNCA, National Museum, Lalit Kala Akademi, National School of Drama, and other affiliated bodies.
Vedic Heritage Portal (Ministry of Culture / IGNCA)
Government project communicating the Vedas' teachings and preserving Vedic heritage; useful for classical knowledge-tradition content.
State tourism & culture departments
State-level tourist site descriptions, festival calendars, regional cultural narratives (e.g. Odisha Tourism, Odisha Culture Department).
Non-government / academic cultural databases
Source
What It Provides
Sahapedia
Open, non-profit online resource on Indian arts/culture/heritage, built with researchers and institutional partners (Ministry of Culture, ASI, UNESCO). Structured across ten domains that closely mirror this project's own category structure; runs a crowdsourced “Maps” cultural-mapping project — a strong model for the contribution + verification workflow.
Sahapedia “My City, My Heritage”
City-level heritage documentation project — a useful structural template for city/region-level entity curation.
